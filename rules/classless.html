Classless (Optional Variant)

On this page

Classless is an optional variant of You-Meet-In-A-Tavern (YMIAT) that removes the class chassis. Instead of picking a class and unlocking its features by level, you spend XP to train individual abilities from a shared pool, at your own pace. Everything else about YMIAT — the three abilities, Wounds and Defense, Lineage, Heritage, Background, Talents, and Magic — works exactly as described elsewhere in the rules unless this page says otherwise.

Open Classless Character Sheet

Use this page instead of Classes

Classless replaces Classes as the character chassis. Everywhere else in the rules that says "your class determines…", read it as "your trained abilities determine…" for a Classless character. This is a Game Master option — agree on it with your table before character creation.

What Changes

  • No classes. Skip the "Choose a Class" step entirely. There is no class list, no per-class proficiencies, and no fixed spellcasting ability tied to a class.
  • Max Wounds grows through training, not levels. Every Classless character starts from the same flat base instead of a class start value, and gains further Max Wounds by training the Toughness ability instead of automatically per character level; see Max Wounds below.
  • Abilities are trained, not granted. Features that would normally come from a class or level (Rage, Sneak Attack, Spellcasting, and so on) are instead learned individually by spending XP; see Trained Abilities.
  • Silver is the everyday coin. Gold is rare and mostly a store of value, not pocket change. Starting funds and equipment prices are in silver pieces (SP); see Currency.
  • Lineage, Heritage, and Background are unchanged — you still choose all three as normal.

Character Creation (Classless)

Follow the same overall flow as standard Character Creation, with the class step replaced:

  1. Character Concept — as usual.
  2. Determine Ability Scores (Fitness, Insight, Willpower) — as usual, using Point-Buy or Standard Array.
  3. Choose a Lineage — as usual.
  4. Choose a Heritage — as usual.
  5. Choose a Background for its skills, tool/language proficiencies, and talent choice. Ignore its listed starting money and equipment package — Classless characters buy their own gear (see Currency).
  6. Spend your starting XP budget (20 XP) on trained abilities from the curated Classless ability list; see Trained Abilities.
  7. Spend your starting silver on equipment from the Gear tables.

Max Wounds

Classless characters calculate Max Wounds without a class start value, replacing standard YMIAT's "+1 Max Wounds per character level" with your trained Toughness level:

Max Wounds (Classless) Max Wounds = 4 + Fitness modifier + Toughness level

Toughness level is 0 until you train it — see Toughness below for how it's trained and what it costs. Everything else about Wounds — how they're inflicted, how they're healed, hearts, weakening, and Death Saves — works exactly as described in Wounds and Scale and Combat.

Trained Abilities (XP Economy)

In standard YMIAT, class features unlock automatically as you level. In Classless, there is no level gate — instead, every ability (from any class, plus general Talents) is drawn from one shared pool, and you unlock it by spending accumulated XP on it directly. Think of it as paying for training time rather than for character sheet space.

As in the core rules (see Level Advancement), a newly trained ability doesn't come online mid-scene — it becomes usable after a long rest or a period of dedicated study or practice (Game Master's discretion), reflecting the time it takes to actually learn it.

Starting XP Budget

At creation, you have a budget of 20 XP to spend on trained abilities from the curated Classless ability list, at the same costs as normal training (see Training Cost below). You can split it across more than one ability if you can afford them. Any XP you don't spend isn't lost — it carries over as banked XP toward whatever you train next.

Major-tier abilities (30 XP) cost more than your entire starting budget and can't be trained at creation. Your first Spellcasting source and style is Core tier (15 XP), not Major, so it fits your starting budget with 5 XP to spare — a starting Classless character can be a spellcaster from level 1.

Curated ability list

See Classless Abilities for the curated list of what's trainable, its costs, and its prerequisites — alongside Toughness, Spellcasting, and Weapon and Armor Proficiency below. It's still growing: more class features and Talents get added as they're converted, so treat anything not yet listed as Game Master judgment territory in the meantime.

Toughness (Leveled Ability)

Most trained abilities are bought once. Toughness is different: it's a leveled ability, trained repeatedly for a scaling effect, the way Ability Score Improvement is repeatable in standard YMIAT. Each level of Toughness you train adds +1 Max Wound (see the formula above), and there is no cap on how many levels you can train.

Training a new level of Toughness costs XP based on the level you're training into:

Toughness Training Cost XP cost = 10 × Toughness level
Toughness LevelMax Wounds BonusXP to Train This LevelCumulative XP Spent
1+110 XP10 XP
2+220 XP30 XP
3+330 XP60 XP
4+440 XP100 XP
5+550 XP150 XP

Toughness is a Trained Ability like any other, so it can be trained from your starting XP budget: level 1 costs 10 XP, leaving 10 XP for something else. Level 2 costs 20 XP more (30 XP total) — already more than the whole starting budget — so most starting characters cap out at Toughness level 1, if they take it at all. It isn't mandatory; a character who never trains it simply keeps Max Wounds = 4 + Fitness modifier.

Spellcasting

Spellcasting is a Trained Ability like any other. Your first source and style costs the Core tier, 15 XP (see Training Cost below) — it fits within your starting XP budget, so Spellcasting can be trained at creation. Training an additional source or style later (multiple traditions of magic at once) is a heavier ask and costs the Major tier, 30 XP, each time.

Prerequisite: a +1 or better modifier in whichever ability powers your spells (see "Casting ability" below). You can't train Spellcasting through an ability that's still +0 or negative.

Training Spellcasting means making three choices:

  • Source of magic — which spell list you draw from: Arcane, Nature, Mystic (includes Divine), or Hex. The Magic page doesn't have a source filter, only class and school filters — as a rule of thumb, use the class filter: Arcane ≈ Wizard/Sorcerer/Artificer spells, Nature ≈ Druid/Ranger spells, Mystic ≈ Cleric/Paladin (Divine) spells, Hex ≈ Warlock/Witch spells. Bard spells can slot into whichever source fits your concept.
  • Casting ability — Insight or Willpower. Its modifier is your spellcasting modifier, used for Spell Power, spell attack rolls and save DCs, and everything else in Magic and Spell Resources, unchanged from the core rules.
  • Casting style — Prepared or Known, below. Training a second source or a second style later is a separate Major (30 XP) purchase each time — it isn't discounted for already being a caster.
Prepared CasterKnown Caster
Cantrips known3 + spellcasting modifier1 + spellcasting modifier
Spells learned at start4 × spellcasting modifier2 × spellcasting modifier
Spells usable at oncespellcasting modifier (re-prepare from your learned spells after a long rest)all spells learned — there's no separate "prepared" pool

Prepared casters can also attempt a learned spell they haven't currently prepared: roll d20 + spellcasting modifier against DC 15 + the spell's level. On a success, cast it spending Spell Power as normal. On a failure, the spell fails and no Spell Power is spent.

Known casters can only cast what they currently know — there's no equivalent to flipping through unprepared spells. A Known caster's spell list is fixed until they train a new spell.

Cantrips are castable at will for both styles: no Spell Power cost, and no roll to cast. Unlike your learned/known spell counts, your cantrip count isn't fixed at training — it recalculates automatically whenever your spellcasting modifier changes (for example after training a level of Ability Score Improvement).

Beyond the starting values above, growing your spell list is a matter of play, not a fixed price: train a new spell for XP, pay a trainer or scribe in coin, or find one in the field (a scroll to copy, a rival's spellbook, a ritual taught by an NPC). The Game Master adjudicates cost and availability case by case. For a Known caster, only training (spending XP) actually adds the new spell to their fixed list — coin or in-fiction discovery can locate a spell to learn, but the XP is still what makes it stick.

Weapon and Armor Proficiency

Weapon proficiency adds your Fitness modifier to the attack roll. Without the matching proficiency, you can still use a weapon, but you don't add Fitness modifier — the attack roll uses only the weapon's plain attack bonus.

Weapon Attack Roll (Classless) d20 + weapon attack bonus + Fitness modifier (if proficient)

Armor proficiency keeps the core Armor and Defense penalties for wearing armor above your trained category — training the abilities below just raises which category you're proficient in. Per step above your proficiency, you gain one level of stacking disadvantage on all d20 rolls made in that armor, including Fitness checks, Defense rolls, and spellcasting rolls for spells with a somatic component.

Training Cost (Other Abilities)

Toughness above has its own fixed cost formula because it's leveled. Every other (one-off) trained ability costs a flat amount of XP. The exact cost per ability is still being tuned alongside the curated list above — treat the table below as a starting default you can adjust for your table, not a final number.

TierSuggested XP CostExample
Minor5 XPA skill/tool proficiency, a small utility feature
Core15 XPRage, Sneak Attack, Bardic Inspiration, your first Spellcasting source and style
Major30 XPExtra Attack, Wild Shape, an additional Spellcasting source or style

Classless runs on its own, smaller XP scale — these costs are roughly a tenth of standard YMIAT's leveling thresholds (see Experience Points), not directly comparable to them. Subclass-style abilities and repeatable Talents (like Ability Score Improvement) can be trained the same way, at Game Master discretion.

Currency: Silver Standard

Classless assumes a lower-magic, more historically grounded economy: silver is the coin ordinary people actually use, and gold is uncommon enough to be a notable find rather than pocket change.

  • Starting funds: every Classless character begins with 100 SP, regardless of Background.
  • Equipment prices: use the SP/CP prices already listed on the Gear page — for example, a shortsword costs 60 SP.
  • Gold pieces (GP) still exist as a rare, high-value coin (roughly 10 SP per GP) for treasure and big-ticket transactions, but new characters don't start with any.

Ignore Background gold amounts

Backgrounds still list legacy starting money in gold pieces (a holdover that hasn't been converted to the silver-standard economy). For Classless characters, ignore that figure entirely and use the flat 100 SP above instead.

Everything Else Stays the Same

Classless only touches the character chassis, ability training, and starting currency. Everything else in the core rules applies unchanged:

  • Core Rules — dice and tests, proficiency and advantage, skills, Resolve, rests.
  • Combat — attacks, Defense, Life/Death Saves.
  • Lineages, Heritages, and Backgrounds — chosen exactly as normal (aside from the equipment override above).
  • Talents — part of the same trained-ability pool as class features.
  • Magic — spells work the same once a character has trained the relevant Spellcasting ability.

Toughness, Spellcasting, Weapon and Armor Proficiency, and a first batch of class-feature-style abilities on Classless Abilities are now defined; the rest of the class/Talent list is still open.
  • Final XP costs for one-off abilities — the tiers in Training Cost are a placeholder (Toughness and Spellcasting have fixed costs, see above).
  • Whether some abilities need prerequisites (e.g. training an upgraded feature before its base version) or a "tier cap" tied to total XP spent, to keep power roughly in line with a same-XP class character.
  • Exact prices for learning spells beyond a caster's starting count.
