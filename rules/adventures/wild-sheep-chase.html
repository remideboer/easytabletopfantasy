The party's attempt to grab a rare afternoon of downtime is interrupted by a frantic sheep equipped with a scroll and a fierce determination to get their attention. This is no mere beast, however, but a wizard fallen victim to an embittered apprentice wielding a Wand of True Polymorph.

Transformed guards are looking to grab themselves a mutton dinner, while the only thing capable of restoring his opposable thumbs lies in the hands of his former pupil and current nemesis. Fortunately, the woolly wizard has found new allies he can rely on, right? Can the heroes put an end to Ahmed Noke's transmutational tyranny and restore an innocent wizard to his true form?

Using This Adventure

The text below is divided into scenes, each covering a location, encounter, or major plot point. Stat blocks are boxed. Read the whole thing before running it — the ending branches on player choice, and Noke's tactics assume you already know his escape plan.

Suggested party readiness: this one-shot assumes a competent, roughly mid-tier party — in standard YMIAT terms, characters around 4th–5th level. For a Classless party there's no direct level equivalent, but a full starting XP budget already spent plus some banked XP from prior play (a trained Toughness level or two, a couple of weapon proficiencies, and a Core-tier ability apiece) is a reasonable bar. Scale Guz, the guards, and Noke up or down with Game Master judgment if your table runs lighter or heavier than that.

Adventure Hooks

Getting the players into this adventure is straightforward. All it requires is that they be spending a few days in a town or city large enough to host a wizard.

If you want to plant seeds in advance, introduce the character of Noke through rumours or idle conversation with shopkeepers and innkeepers, who view him with fear and awe.

Baaaa-d News

The adventure begins as the party idle away an afternoon. They might be enjoying a drink at a tavern, resting at their base, or simply walking down the street without a care in the world. No matter where they are, it soon becomes clear that something quite odd is happening.

There is a sound of clattering hooves, surprised yelps, and a frantic bleating. Before the party can react, a sheep bounds towards them.

The beast appears to be a regular sheep in every way — fluffy white coat, black face, curled horns — but it is carrying an elaborate scroll in its mouth.

The sheep tries to get close to the most magically-gifted member of the party and waves the scroll at them, letting them know it should be reached for.

A wax seal purports that it is a Scroll of Speak with Animals (Modified). If a character says this out loud — to explain to other party members, for example — the sheep appears to nod and bleat enthusiastically.

Should they use the scroll (simply reading it aloud is enough to activate the spell — see Speak with Animals, 1st level), all of them hear the sheep's baaing instantly morphs into cultured, elven-accented Common, albeit with a slight hint of a bleat.

After establishing that the player can understand what it's saying, the sheep introduces itself as Finethir Shinebright, a wizard in dire need of aid. If the players are willing to listen, he explains that he recognizes them as adventurers and that he is in dire need of their help. Specifically, he wants them to help take back an extremely powerful magical artefact from a dangerous, possibly insane, wizard.

He will attempt to tell his story, but shortly after he starts the sound of howling fills the air…

Shepherds, Crooks

Paraphrase

A loud howling fills the air, accompanied by the sound of angry yells and the occasional scream that seem to be drawing closer and closer.

The cause quickly becomes apparent as a huge Half-Orc swaggers towards you, pushing his way through the crowd without a care for anybody standing in his way. In front of him walk three large wolves wearing iron collars, while a hulking figure in a dirty brown cloak travels in his wake with footfalls loud enough to be heard over the ruckus.

The Half-Orc sets his small eyes on you and strides forward with one hand resting on the hilt of a greatsword.

"That sheep's Master Noke's… he desires to have it back."

The Half-Orc, Guz, is accompanied by a trio of collared Wolves with strangely intelligent eyes, while the huge figure behind him is actually a polymorphed Brown Bear. He demands that he be given "Master Noke's sheep," which he claims has escaped and is of great sentimental value to his master. Any mention of Shinebright's true nature is met with hollow, mirthless laughter.

Guz is not a bright fellow, but he is very loyal and determined to complete his task. His trackers know that Shinebright is there, no matter how well the party tries to hide him, and will not take no for an answer.

While he prefers violence and intimidation, he is also willing to offer bribes and promises of magical favours if that looks like the best option. If he feels like he's not making progress, Guz will simply attack without warning — the wolves follow up while the cloaked figure lets its hood fall and runs into combat with a terrible roar.

The exact details of any battle depend on the location. In an inn, tables and overturned chairs litter the floor; in a street, frightened passers-by form zones of difficult terrain.

All the beasts have an intelligent human mind contained within them and act intelligently. Guz is happy to charge into the front line alongside the Brown Bear. Meanwhile two of the Wolves aim to cut round behind the party, and the third — the one tasked with actually grabbing Shinebright — fights as the sturdier, cannier Pony below instead.

They intend to capture Shinebright, not kill him. If Guz captures him, he and his allies set off for Noke's home. The party will have to decide if they follow, potentially launching a chase scene.

Roleplaying Shinebright

Arrogant, self-obsessed, and filled with an inflated sense of his own ego, Finethir Shinebright is one of the people worst suited to being transformed into a sheep. Still, no matter his form, the former elven wizard manages to exude an air of fussy, bookish intellectualism.

Despite his flaws, he is generally good-hearted. Assuming he notices and it doesn't inconvenience him too much, he'll try to help others where possible, though he will loudly complain of their inability to grasp concepts as simple as poly-planal transweave theory.

Once upon a time the local nobles, merchants, and arcanists spoke the name Finethir Shinebright with respect, awe, and grudging admiration. A master of transmutation, he would regularly be called upon for grand tasks such as clearing blocked roads by turning massive boulders into rabbits. Two years as a sheep have taught him very little humility, but a lot about grass. If he returns to his natural form, Shinebright is a willow-thin high elf with straw-coloured hair and an extremely long nose.

After the Dust Settles

Once Noke's goons have been defeated, distracted, or otherwise dealt with, Shinebright pleads with the party. Without their help, he is doomed — Noke has many guards still working for him, and eventually they will find him.

If they're feeling mercenary, he reminds the party that in his true form he is a powerful and wealthy wizard, and promises great rewards he's more than willing to commit to deals he knows he can't actually fulfil.

In any case, Shinebright explains:

  • Until two years ago he owned and worked out of a tower on the outskirts of town, specialising in transmutation magic. His most prized possession — and probably the key to his success — was an incredibly rare Wand of True Polymorph.
  • One fateful night he woke from his meditative trance to find his apprentice, Ahmed Noke, standing over him, clutching the wand. Shinebright demanded to know what the boy was doing, but the only noise he could produce was an angry "baaaah."
  • The wizard became a virtual prisoner in his own garden. He was forced to graze on nothing but grass and buttercups while hungry wolves and other beasts — actually polymorphed guards — looked on.
  • Last night he felt hope for the first time in many months, when Noke left his home without closing the door. Shinebright snuck in, made his way to an old bookshelf, and stole the Scroll of Speak with Animals. He then rushed into town with the scroll clenched between his teeth, searching desperately for someone able to help — and found the party.

Roleplaying Guz

A hulking brute of a half-orc, Guz views violence and intimidation as the only sensible methods for achieving his goals. He also assumes most people feel the same way and prefers to get his own violence in first.

Though to most outside observers he may appear evil, he is incredibly loyal to Noke — who genuinely doesn't care what a creature looks like on the outside — and owes him unswerving devotion.

Guz isn't particularly intelligent and can be easily confused by quick-talking foes, though this usually just makes him angrier. However, he is also fond of trying to use long, complicated words he has picked up from Noke — though the extent to which he actually understands what they mean is probably up for debate.

  • In order to turn back into his original form, Shinebright needs another dose of True Polymorph. Which means he needs access to his old wand.
  • His former apprentice still dwells in Shinebright's old home, located just southeast of town. He keeps the wand on him at all times and only leaves if he absolutely has to.
  • He knows his old home's layout intimately, and is more than happy to describe it in endless detail.

The House in the Woods

The path to Noke's tower cuts off from a main road a few miles out of town, darting off between a gap in the bushes. It is a well-trodden route that soon begins to weave its way between tall oak trees.

Anyone who examines the path for tracks will easily see the impression of many feet and paws, with the most fresh sets appearing to match up with the group led by Guz.

The track continues through sparse woodland for perhaps a mile without any sign of habitation. Unless they know what to look for, the party may be somewhat surprised by the view that awaits them when Shinebright's old house appears through the treetops.

Paraphrase

Rather than stone or glass, the house before you seems to have been formed from the living branches of four sturdy oak trees. These have been shaped and woven to create three thick platforms.

The lowest of these platforms is roughly 40 ft. across and sits about 10 ft. from the ground. The only obvious route up is a gentle slope formed of roots and branches that connects roughly with the main path. Branches curl around its base, creating a rough bowl shape around it. From where you stand it's possible to see flowers and small trees growing around its edge.

By far the largest of the three platforms is the middle one, which looks to be around 60 ft. across. It is about 20 ft. above the ground and is fully enclosed with a wall formed of twisting branches. You can see evenly spaced, window-sized gaps, as well as what appears to be a door at the point closest to the garden platform.

The final, tallest platform is roughly 30 ft. above the ground, and is much smaller than the others. It looks to be linked to the central platform by another small slope.

Scattered beneath the platforms are two small wooden huts and a large outhouse.

When the party arrives at the compound, there is a trio of Apes either sleeping or playing with an oversized pair of dice, with iron greatswords stuck into the ground next to them. A Brown Bear is in the outhouse, taking care of business.

The door to the central platform is almost always locked, requiring a DC14 Fitness (Athletics) check to break down or a DC12 Fitness (Thieves' Tools) check to pick. Inside, the area is divided into three sections: one is a combination library and lab, one a sitting room, and one a dining room/kitchen. Untidy bookshelves line much of the outer walls, and worktables are covered with stacks of ink-spattered notes and complicated anatomical diagrams of various beasts and monsters.

The tallest platform functions as a bedroom for Noke, containing little beyond a wardrobe, a large wooden bed, and a cluttered dressing table. The huts contain one or two beds each, a few of which are oversized, as well as the general clutter of the common men who occupy them.

Roleplaying Noke

Noke once hero-worshipped Shinebright and served as his apprentice for many years. However, as time dragged on and on there was never any change in their relationship.

Decades passed and still the master transmuter treated him like a child, expected to cook, clean, and recite answers by rote. When Noke pressed him on it, Shinebright would explain that this was how he had been taught, and didn't seem to register Noke's claims that, as a human, he couldn't spare a century to serve out an apprenticeship.

At the same time, Noke began to realise that much of Shinebright's acclaim came not from his own abilities — extensive as they were — but from the Wand of True Polymorph he wielded.

Eventually he cracked, and turned on his former master before setting himself up as a master wizard. However, a combination of guilt and suddenly unstoppered ambition spelled bad news for his mental state. He is paranoid that someone will attack him as he attacked Shinebright, and rarely sleeps. This paranoia has led him to invest in a troop of guards, many of whom he has transmuted into stronger, more bestial forms.

All the beasts still have an intelligent human mind and act intelligently — they'll call for help, retreat toward Noke, and try to buy him time to prepare rather than simply dying in place.

Confronting Noke

Noke is naturally paranoid, and Shinebright's recent escape hasn't exactly improved his outlook. This makes luring him away from the safety of his tower an extremely difficult task, though not an impossible one. His lust for fame and power slightly outweighs his fear of being unseated — for the time being, at least. A very skilled set of liars with a sufficiently devious plan and a good helping of luck may be able to tempt him with the offer of a particularly prestigious, lucrative contract.

Noke is working furiously on new spells in the central platform. If he notices the party approach, he'll order them to "return the sheep — in return I will not destroy you all." If they talk, he'll reveal his reasons for hating Shinebright and boast disjointedly of his own achievements. He'll also ask if they killed "my man, Guz," and be visibly upset if told he's dead. Should they refuse to return Shinebright, he'll order his men to attack.

The most important part of any fight that breaks out is Noke, who is offensively weak but can buff his allies dramatically. Once the Brown Bear emerges from the outhouse, he casts Haste on it (3rd level, 3 Spell Power: doubles its Speed, +2 Defense, advantage on Fitness saves, and an extra Attack/Dash/Disengage/Hide/Utilize each turn — used here in place of the original's Enlarge/Reduce, reflavored as brute transmutation). He'll supplement his minions' attacks with Ray of Frost (cantrip, no Spell Power cost: ranged spell attack, 1 Cold Wound and −10 ft. Speed until the start of his next turn on a hit), focusing on keeping up Concentration on Haste.

The Apes fight to the death and flee once they fall below half health. Once it's clear the fight is lost, or a player simply gets too close to him, Noke casts Expeditious Retreat (1st level, 1 Spell Power: lets him Dash immediately, then keep Dashing every turn on top of his normal action while it lasts) and flees into the central platform, locking the door behind him. He'll move to his bedroom as fast as he can and cast True Polymorph on the largest thing he can find — his bed.

Ahmed Noke

Medium humanoid (human), neutral evil

Defense 12   Max Wounds 6   Speed 30 ft.

Fitness +2   Insight +3   Willpower +0

Perception 11   Languages Common, Elven, Draconic

Spellcasting. Noke's spellcasting ability is Insight (spell save DC 14). He has the following spells prepared: Ray of Frost (cantrip), Expeditious Retreat (1st level), Haste (3rd level). He also has one charge of the Modified Wand of True Polymorph — see below.

Actions

Dagger. Melee or Ranged Weapon Attack: 16 to save, reach 5 ft. or range 20/60 ft., one target. Hit: 1 hit of piercing damage.

Three rounds after he runs away, he bursts from the roof of his bedroom riding the Bed Dragon Wyrmling — a beast that looks like a dragon carved from wood, with billowing bedsheets for wings and a tail that ends in a soft pillow. If the party are in the main living area, he'll crash through the ceiling and attack.

The Wyrmling isn't that smart and uses its Splinter Breath as often as possible, simply attacking the closest target when it isn't available. It will, however, prioritise enemies that use fire against it. While riding the Wyrmling, Noke uses Ray of Frost to slow down any party members trying to retreat.

Should the Wyrmling be lost — either to damage or because Noke loses Concentration — it turns back into a thoroughly beaten-up old bed.

Desperate and unwilling to let his old master win, a babbling Noke will then attempt to use the wand on himself, intending to transmute his form into that of some monstrous creature. However, the over-used wand crackles, fizzes, and malfunctions with a loud bang, turning Noke into a Gibbering Mouther — a misshapen pile of flesh, organs, and facial features. The sad creature attacks mindlessly, babbling incoherently from dozens of mouths, until completely destroyed.

Even so, when Noke leaves he takes all of his remaining bodyguards with him and is extremely quick to suspect the party of a double-cross if there's any hint of one. He has his wand to hand at all times; should he be given enough time to cast it, Noke uses a charge of True Polymorph to transmute some nearby item into a Wyrmling using roughly the same stats as the Bed Dragon Wyrmling above — depending on what it was made from, it may not have the same flavour and might need its resistances/vulnerabilities tweaked slightly.

The Modified Wand of True Polymorph

Formed of a long, thin twig taken from an oak tree, this wand holds 1d4−1 charges of True Polymorph (9th level), with a minimum of 1. It can be used by anybody with a spellcasting ability who has spent at least one hour attuning to it. Charges reset each dawn.

In order to increase how many times the spell can be cast from it, the wand has been fitted with a bulbous, rune-inscribed iron band and has cracks running along its length.

If inspected with a DC15 Insight (Arcana) check, this reveals that the modifications have left the wand extremely unreliable. Each time a charge is used, the user must pass a DC17 Insight (Arcana) check.

  • On a success, the spell casts normally, and the DC needed to use it permanently increases by 1.
  • On a failure by five or less, the spell instead transmutes the target into a Gibbering Mouther (stat block above) — a hideous lump of seemingly random limbs, organs, and facial features, which attacks the nearest target mindlessly, babbling in dozens of broken voices. This transformation can't be undone by any spell lower than 9th level and can't be cancelled by ending Concentration. The target can make a saving throw against this effect the same way it would against a regular casting of True Polymorph.
  • On a failure by more than five, in addition to the above, the wand explodes violently: 1 hit of Force damage to all creatures within 10 ft. for each charge remaining in the wand. This destroys the wand.

Big Choices

Should the players recover the wand, it becomes immediately obvious that it's damaged. Anyone proficient in Arcana can tell it's been modified so it can be used more often, but overuse has rendered it extremely unreliable.

If they wish to speak to Shinebright, a quick search of the central platform will turn up another Scroll of Speak with Animals. At the sight of his former apprentice's ruined corpse, Shinebright may even undergo some realisation of how poorly he treated Noke, and turn somber despite his triumph.

Even if he's made aware of the dangers associated with his old wand, Shinebright is still willing to risk his life to be turned back into an elf. However, he is also open to persuasion if the party try to talk him out of it. A compelling argument and a DC15 Willpower (Persuasion) check is enough to make the former wizard reconsider his options.

Before undergoing the risk of having the spell performed on him, Shinebright will ask that word be sent to his old college if he does die.

If the party goes ahead with the spell and it succeeds, Shinebright congratulates the party heartily. He'll have to be reminded of anything he actually promised while he was still a sheep, and can't actually pay any financial reward, as Noke was virtually bankrupt and had sold most items to pay for research materials and guards. He will, however, be willing to perform transmutations of up to 5th level for them, charging just enough to cover the components. He'll also take in any polymorphed guards who fled, promising to work towards returning them to their natural shapes.

If the party goes ahead with the spell and it fails, Shinebright dies permanently. The party is free to honour his final wish and leave the tower more-or-less intact, or loot it to their hearts' content. The tower is surprisingly empty of anything of benefit to non-transmuters, however, with valuables restricted to expensive lab equipment and arcane paraphernalia — a rare find worth roughly 1,000 GP, the kind of big-ticket treasure Classless's silver-standard economy sets aside gold for, not the everyday SP. If the players wish, they can also take the entire tower complex as their own, though this may cause trouble when the local nobles try to pay Noke a visit and find him missing.

If the party refuses to perform the spell, Shinebright accepts but won't give up his hopes entirely. He takes back his old home and works towards a way to remove his curse. In any case, he heads to the bedroom and slips on one of his old robes, which he wears until he's transformed back to his natural shape. Though he'll be upset with them, Shinebright acknowledges that he owes the party a large debt.

In any case, any adventurers that help Shinebright deal with Noke recieve the Rewards below.

Not Like This…

A stealthy party may be able to steal the wand while Noke sleeps, or a combination of lucky rolls and inventive tactics may allow a player to disarm the wizard mid-fight. Should this happen, don't force the story back towards the "ideal path," but allow it to develop naturally.

One thing you must do, however, is try to provide at least a hint of the dangers associated with the modified wand before the players try to use it. Possibly it sparks unnaturally when a character touches it, or Noke himself may feel honour-bound to warn them.

Rewards

Award XP using the Classless Earning XP table (0/1/2/3/5 for Trivial/Easy/Moderate/Hard/Near Impossible), scene by scene rather than as one lump sum at the end — the original's flat "500 XP" doesn't translate to Classless's much smaller scale. A reasonable read of this adventure's difficulty:

SceneSuggested DifficultyXP
Talking down or fighting Guz and his guardsHard3
Clearing or sneaking past the house's guardsHard3
Confronting Noke and the Bed Dragon WyrmlingNear Impossible5
Resolving Shinebright's fate (talked down, spell succeeds, or spell fails)Moderate2

That's roughly 13 XP for the whole one-shot if the party goes through every scene — enough to train a Core-tier ability (15 XP) with a little left over, or several Minor ones, which feels about right for a single session's payout on Classless's scale. Adjust freely; it's Game Master judgment either way.
